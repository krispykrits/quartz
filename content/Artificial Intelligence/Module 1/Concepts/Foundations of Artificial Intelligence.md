# Foundations of Artificial Intelligence
_Source slides: 12-23_

| Discipline | Lecture contribution |
|---|---|
| Philosophy | logic, mind, action |
| Mathematics | logic, probability, computation |
| Economics | utility, games, MDPs |
| Neuroscience | neurons, brain data |
| Psychology | cognition, behavior |
| Computer engineering | hardware, software |
| Control theory | feedback, optimization |
| Linguistics | language, meaning |

## Philosophy
Formal reasoning, materialism, empiricism, induction, and knowledge-to-action. Slide 14's planning diagram moves from goal → subgoals/preconditions → executable actions.

## Mathematics
Formal logic, probability, computability, and complexity. See [[Ch01 Mathematics#Bayes Rule]].

## Economics
Preferences, utility, decision theory, game theory, operations research, and MDPs. See [[Ch01 Mathematics#Bellman Optimality Equation]].

## Neuroscience
Brains demonstrate intelligence emerging from electrochemical components, but raw brain/computer comparisons are not a theory of intelligence.

## Psychology
Cognitive psychology uses internal representations, beliefs, goals, and mental models. Kenneth Craik's small-scale model simulates alternatives before action.

## Computer Engineering
AI depends on hardware/software, parallelism, GPUs/TPUs, specialized accelerators, and sometimes lower precision arithmetic.

## Control Theory
Feedback and error correction connect self-regulating systems to rational-agent AI.

## Linguistics
Syntax alone is insufficient; language understanding requires world knowledge, context, goals, discourse, symbols, structures, and probabilities.
