# Neural and Probabilistic AI
_Source slides: 25, 27, 32-36_

## Early Neural Ideas
McCulloch-Pitts threshold neurons, Hebbian learning, and Rosenblatt's perceptron. The perceptron has a convergence theorem for linearly separable data but cannot represent XOR with a single linear boundary.

## Backpropagation Revival
In the mid-1980s, multilayer connectionist models learned distributed internal representations by adjusting parameters to reduce prediction error.

## Probabilistic Turn
From the late 1980s, AI increasingly adopted probability, statistics, decision theory, and empirical evaluation.

## Bayesian Networks
Directed edges encode direct probabilistic dependence. Conditional independence allows compact factorization. See [[Ch01 Mathematics#Bayesian Network Factorization]].

## Big Data
The Web and cheap storage enabled massive datasets. More data can sometimes outperform small algorithmic tweaks. See [[Ch01 Mathematics#Learning-Curve Intuition]].

## Deep Learning
Multiple adjustable layers learn internal representations. The lecture attributes the 2011-2012 surge to data, hardware, and training methods. See [[Ch01 Mathematics#Deep-Learning Layer Computation]].

> [!warning]
> Deep learning still depends on assumptions, data quality, objectives, and evaluation.
