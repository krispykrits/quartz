# Four Views of Artificial Intelligence
_Source slides: 4-8_

| | Human-centered | Rationality-centered |
|---|---|---|
| Thought | Thinking humanly | Thinking rationally |
| Behavior | Acting humanly | Acting rationally |

## Thinking Humanly
Cognitive modeling treats programs as testable models of human thought. Matching the correct answer is insufficient; mechanism, timing, and errors should match human data.

## Acting Humanly
The Turing test evaluates whether written machine behavior is distinguishable from a person's. The total Turing test adds perception and physical action.

## Thinking Rationally
The laws-of-thought tradition formalizes valid inference. The lecture's limitation is that uncertainty requires probability and correct belief still does not determine action.

## Acting Rationally
A rational agent selects actions that achieve the best outcome or best expected outcome under uncertainty. It may use inference, search, planning, learning, reflexes, or heuristics.

> [!important]
> Human-centered approaches are partly empirical sciences; rational approaches are mathematical and engineering-oriented.

## Why AIMA Centers Rational Action
It defines intelligence by a performance objective rather than by imitation of human behavior or cognition.
