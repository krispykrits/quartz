# Chapter 3 - Why and How

## Why can subthreshold inputs still cause a spike?
Because PSPs integrate spatially and temporally; their sum may reach threshold.

## Why does myelin help propagation?
It limits charge leakage, while nodes of Ranvier regenerate the action potential.

## Why does inhibition change classification?
It reduces net activation and can prevent threshold crossing.

## Why is the basic Hebb rule unstable?
The lecture notes that it lacks an intrinsic stabilizing term.

## How do gated Hebbian rules stabilize?
When the gate is active, \([y-w]\) moves the weight toward bounded activity.

## Why can equal spike counts produce different responses?
Timing, coincidence, and which inputs fired can differ.

## Why does center-surround processing detect contrast?
Center excitation and surround inhibition suppress uniform input and emphasize spatial differences.

## Why does population coding help represent direction?
Different cells have different preferred directions; the joint activity pattern carries direction information.

## Why are large-scale integration and synchronization important?
The lecture shows that local activity magnitude alone may be similar while cross-regional coherence differs strongly with perception.
